# Landing Page Best Practices: 7 Things a High-Converting Page Needs

You're paying for ads, the clicks are coming in, and the inquiries aren't. More often than not, the problem is the page people arrive on, not the campaign itself.

A landing page has one job: getting the visitor to take one action. Anything that doesn't serve that action gets in its way. These are the landing page best practices worth checking before you launch, and that every high-converting landing page has in common.

## 1. One message that matches the ad

The visitor clicked an ad that promised something specific. The page should carry on with exactly that message, from the headline to the button. An ad for "same-day AC repair" that leads to your general home page loses people in the first second.

## 2. A headline that says what the customer gets

Not "Welcome" and not your business name. The headline should answer "what's in it for me?" in one sentence. Someone who clicked an ad for AC repair isn't looking for your company history or a list of every service. They want to know you can come today, roughly what it costs and how to book.

## 3. One clear call to action

One button, one action: request a quote, book a call, send a message or buy. Every extra link, menu or "learn more about us" is a way out of the page.

## 4. A short form, or a chat button

Every extra form field means fewer people complete it. Ask only for what you need to get back to them, usually a name and a phone number or email. In markets where people message businesses on WhatsApp, a chat button often works better than a form. What should happen once the details arrive is covered in the [guide to lead follow-up](stop-losing-leads).

## 5. Proof

Why should they believe you? Real work, a screenshot, a review from a named customer. One genuine piece of proof is worth more than ten promises, and visitors can tell the difference quickly.

## 6. Speed, especially on mobile

Most visitors arrive on a phone, sometimes on a weak connection. A slow page loses them before they've seen anything. Google's Core Web Vitals give a useful target: the main content should load within 2.5 seconds.

A simple test: open the page on your phone over mobile data, not office Wi-Fi. If nothing useful appears within a few seconds, some visitors have already hit back. The usual culprit is one heavy image at the top of the page.

Speed matters for your ad costs too. In Google Ads, landing page experience is one of the three components of Quality Score, alongside expected clickthrough rate and ad relevance.

## 7. Measurement

Without measurement you can't tell whether the page works. Every form submission and every chat click should count as a conversion and be tied to the campaign that brought it. That's covered in depth in the article on [conversion tracking](conversion-tracking).

## How to tell if your landing page is working

Open the page on your phone as if you were a customer who's never heard of you, and ask:

- Within five seconds, is it clear what's on offer and who it's for?
- Is the next step obvious?
- How many taps and fields does it take to send an inquiry?
- Does the page load quickly?

If the answer to any of these isn't good, that's where to start.

## Frequently asked questions

**What's the difference between a landing page and a website?**
A website presents your whole business and lets visitors browse. A landing page focuses on one offer and one action, and usually serves a specific campaign.

**Do I need a separate page for each campaign?**
When the messages are different, yes. Ads for two different services work better when each lands on a page about exactly that service.

**How long does it take to build one?**
A focused page is relatively quick to build. What takes time is sharpening the message, and that's where the effort pays off.
