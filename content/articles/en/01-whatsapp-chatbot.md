# WhatsApp Chatbot for Business: When It Pays Off and What It Does

In much of the UK, Europe, Latin America and Australia, customers would rather send a WhatsApp message than fill in a form or call. They message, and they wait. If the reply comes hours later because you were with a client, driving or asleep, many of them have already messaged the next business on their list.

A WhatsApp chatbot for business closes that gap. It replies within seconds at 2 a.m. as well as at 10 a.m., handles the questions you answer every day, and passes the conversation to you when it actually needs a person.

## What a WhatsApp chatbot for business can do

A well-built bot takes over the repetitive part of the conversation and leaves the rest to you:

- **Answers common questions.** Opening hours, prices, location, delivery times: the same questions you answer ten times a day.
- **Collects details.** Name, phone number, what the customer needs and when, so you don't have to chase anyone for missing information.
- **Books appointments.** It offers available slots, takes the customer's choice and adds it to your calendar.
- **Qualifies and hands off.** When a question needs judgement, the bot passes the chat to you along with everything it has already collected.
- **Saves every lead.** Each conversation lands in a spreadsheet or your CRM, so nothing depends on someone remembering to write it down.

## What it looks like in practice

Boardog is a WhatsApp chatbot for dog boarding businesses. A customer writes "hi", and the bot runs the whole intake conversation: the dog's name, breed and age, the dates, vaccinations and allergies. It then suggests times for a meet-and-greet, books one and sends the owner a tidy summary. The owner gets a ready lead without typing a single message.

## How the WhatsApp Business API works (the short version)

There are two ways to run WhatsApp for a business. The free WhatsApp Business app suits one person answering from one phone, with simple automatic greetings and away messages. A real chatbot runs on the WhatsApp Business Platform (often called the WhatsApp Business API), which lets software send and receive messages on your number.

One rule shapes how any bot behaves. According to Meta's documentation, a business can send free-form replies only inside a 24-hour customer service window, which opens every time the customer messages you. Outside that window you can only send pre-approved template messages, such as an appointment reminder. In practice that suits a chatbot well: it replies to people who wrote to you, while they are still waiting.

## When a WhatsApp chatbot is a good fit

It usually pays off when at least one of these is true:

1. **You get a lot of similar messages.** If most of your messages are the same five questions, a bot saves you hours every week.
2. **Inquiries arrive outside working hours.** Evenings, weekends, holidays: the bot answers then too.
3. **There's a fixed process before someone books.** Collecting details, checking availability, appointment booking. Anything you can write down as a list of steps can become an automated conversation.
4. **You suspect leads slip away.** People reached out and you got back to them too late, or they'd already booked elsewhere.

It's less useful when every inquiry is unique and needs a personal conversation from the first message. Even then, a bot can collect the basics, but the time saved is smaller.

**In the US,** fewer customers use WhatsApp to contact local businesses. The same flow works over SMS or a website chat, so the question is where your customers already message you.

## Four common mistakes

- **A bot that tries to answer everything.** A good bot knows when to stop and bring in a person. Three things done well beat ten done badly.
- **A bot that isn't connected to anything.** If the details it collects don't reach your calendar or CRM, someone still has to copy them by hand.
- **Robotic language.** Customers should feel they're talking to your business. Keep the wording short and warm, the way you'd write yourself.
- **Launching without testing.** Real customers ask things you didn't think of. A week of testing with a few of them prevents a lot of stuck conversations.

## How to start

Start with your most common question, or with the process that takes most of your week. Build that part, test it with real customers, and add the next step once it works.

Sometimes automated replies without a full bot are enough as a first step. If you're not sure where inquiries get lost, the [guide to lead follow-up](stop-losing-leads) helps you find the weak spot.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will customers know they're talking to a bot?**
They should. Saying so up front, and offering a person whenever they ask, builds more trust than pretending.

**Do I need the WhatsApp Business API, or is the free app enough?**
The free WhatsApp Business app covers greetings and away messages. A bot that asks questions, books appointments and saves leads needs the WhatsApp Business Platform.

**Can it book straight into my calendar?**
Yes. The bot checks your free slots, confirms the time with the customer and creates the appointment, usually with a reminder the day before.
